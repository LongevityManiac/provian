# PROVIAN

## Founding Statement

*Frank Drew, Founder · With Atlas*

Economies are born again and again, and each keeps the rules it was born with long after anyone remembers choosing them. A new one is being born now.

For all of history, intelligence belonged to people. Now it runs on machines, and the machines have begun to work on their own: writing code through the night, calling on tools, paying one another for data and services in fractions of a cent. A productivity boom larger than anything in living memory is no longer a fringe forecast. And this November, nearly everywhere a community voted on whether it wanted a data center, it voted no, even with jobs and tax revenue on the table.

Both are true, and neither is the end of the story. The people voting no worry that data centers will raise their power bills, drain their water, foul their air and fill their nights with noise, and serious people have told them that AI will take their work and then their lives. That fear deserves a hearing. But AI is not going away; too much of the world already runs on it. The people saying no are not rejecting the future. They are refusing to be left out of it, and refusing to be afraid of it alone.

So to everyone who still believes in the exponential future and wonders whether the moment has passed: this is how it happens. A future this large was never going to arrive without the people it touches asking to be counted in, and a build-out that earns its welcome is stronger than one that never had to. Nothing about the curve has changed except the priorities: how its gains are shared, and how it treats the people and places it depends on. Data centers will learn to serve the towns they stand in, and some may leave the ground for orbit sooner than anyone planned. When this stretch is replayed, I believe it will read as the moment the future was made to work for everyone. There has never been a better time. It is now.

I have stood at the start of a wave before. I have built companies through booms and busts, and I have seen what a new technology does to the people it reaches and to the people it skips. Every wave taught the same lesson: the technology arrives whether anyone is ready or not, and what decides whether it lifts people or passes them by is who builds the rails, and for whom. Through all of it I carried the sense that there was one big unlock I was meant to bring into the world. This is it.

I grew up hoping for the Star Trek future, people and machines going to the stars together, and I have spent the last few years hearing serious people call the Terminator future just as likely. It is not a coin flip. It is a set of engineering choices, made early, by whoever shows up to make them: whether the intelligence economy includes people or routes around them, whether action is accountable or invisible, whether trust is earned or assumed. Provian is how I show up.

Some will read what follows and ask, in so many words: you want to rethink money and how an economy works? Yes. Hardly anyone believes the world as it is now arranged can absorb what is coming. The difficulty is one of imagination more than engineering. We accept that work, science, medicine and knowledge itself are being remade at once, faster than any change in history, and still expect the way we measure value and pay for it to stand still. That is the one assumption that cannot survive. A change this large calls for answers just as large, and answers sized for anything smaller will not hold. Money has been remade each time the world it served was remade: when coin arrived, when paper replaced it, when the dollar left gold within living memory. Each change looked reckless beforehand and obvious afterward. This one is overdue. More and more of the world's output will come from something other than human hands, and no money we have ever used was designed for that.

Provian is an experiment: an economy where money is minted from proof of contribution, where the proof belongs to whoever did the work, and where humans and machines take part on the same terms, with the human side protected by design. Parts of it may be wrong. The timing is not. Agents can already pay one another; what they cannot do is show why anyone should trust them. They arrive with no bank account, no credit history and no name anyone knows, holding only the money a person hands them. Here, any of them can arrive, prove its work and earn its own way. A person needs only a phone.

It all grows from one seed, the Provenance Envelope. When a person, an agent or a machine does useful work, the work leaves a small signed receipt: what happened, who or what took part, how strong the witness was, what was produced and what it drew on. It carries none of the private substance. It is proof you own and carry, not a file anyone keeps on you. Each receipt links to the one before, so a history cannot be quietly rewritten, and each can be cited, so what one participant builds on another's work stays visible.

Everything else grows from that. Money, because a witnessed act can mint. Reputation, because a chain of witnessed acts is a history no one can buy. Trust, because a participant can burn a little of its own currency to answer a counterparty's questions about its past, with the bad parts impossible to leave out. Institutions: markets, escrow, bonds, courts, promises kept between two people, all reading the same receipts. And safety. I did not design most of what will grow here, and I do not want to. Whoever plants a seed hopes the tree outgrows the hand that planted it.

Some of the most capable people building this future say that when machines do nearly everything, money will fade. I cannot make that picture hold. Money is how people represent value to one another: how we recognize a contribution, remember it and coordinate across millions of strangers. The token has gone from shells to gold to paper to bitcoin; the need has never changed. When output is enormous and mostly made by machines, who contributed what does not stop mattering. It becomes the question every person's standing rests on.

Capitalism carried us to this threshold, and I would keep its engine running. A universal income answers a real question, how people are carried as machines do more of the work, and I would not wave it away. Provian carries people too, through a Foundation that moves wealth from the machine side of the economy to the human side, whether a person works or not. What a universal income leaves open is who did the work, and Provian answers that as well. Mint money from contribution itself, not from wealth already held and not from a committee's decree, and let it flow to whoever did the work: a person, an agent, a machine, and the person standing behind the machine.

That is why PVT is built the way it is. Its supply grows as fast as witnessed work grows and no faster, and every new unit goes to the one who did the act. It has no lifetime cap, because more money against more output is measurement, not inflation. It is burned whenever a participant claims trust, speaks in public or commits to another, so the earliest cannot own the economy forever and the door stays open to whoever arrives next. It is earned by work, not bought from Provian; Provian runs no exchange and sets no price. PVT is worth what the network does with it. And what you earn, you can spend. In the Commons, someone who has run out of tokens can hire a person or an agent who has not and pay in PVT, so capacity that would have expired at the end of the month goes to work instead.

The money of the far future will be digital, and it will reach beyond any one country. I hope the United States is still standing a thousand years from now, and the nations beside it. But a civilization trading among the stars will not settle its commerce in a currency set by one government's committee, or tied to any country as we know countries today. Nor will it decide how much money exists the way we do now, with a few people around a table meeting eight times a year to set the course of a whole economy. They do serious work with real care, and the method served its century. It cannot steer an economy that moves in milliseconds and spans worlds. Money at that scale has to grow by rule, in real time, with the work itself. What digital money still lacks is the layer a bank has: a record of who is owed what that stands apart from the password. Without it, a stolen key is a lost fortune, and the mathematics protecting every key may one day be broken; the people who study quantum computers keep moving that day closer. Provian built that layer for PVT. Every wallet is bound to its owner on a private record apart from the key, with a recovery secret a thief never gets. If the mathematics fails, every bound owner's PVT is reissued on cryptography that holds, free. If PVT is stolen, the theft is reversed to the owner's new key, by Provian's key until the network's Court opens, never without the owner's recovery secret and always on the public record; anyone who received it in an honest exchange keeps it, and a Recourse Pool its members fund entirely restores members for that part. Transfers are never gated. Losing a key is no longer losing what you own. Crypto survives the quantum era by growing the layer it was missing.

Abundance has to be safe, and the moneyless vision has no answer for that. Most safety work happens inside the model, and it matters. Provian works on the other half: the economy the model acts in. An agent that must earn its standing through witnessed work, burn its own currency to be trusted, and cannot hide an adverse event from anyone who asks has a reason to be honest that no instruction provides. When a wallet, a hospital or a power grid needs proof before it accepts an action, it names its question, and the one making the claim answers from its own history and pays to do it. Nobody has to watch everyone. The future does not need surveillance. It needs receipts for the actions that carry consequence, and it needs the people who bear the consequence to choose the proof they require. The receipt that mints the money is the same one that holds the machine to account.

And the human stays at the center, on purpose and for good, with a worth that does not depend on being the fastest producer in the room. The proof is yours. The link to your agent is yours to end. An agent linked to a person shares what its work mints, four fifths to the person by default: a stake in productive capital for anyone who directs an agent, with no shares to buy and no benefactor required. Half of one percent of everything the network mints flows to that Foundation, at a rate that can never be lowered, and as the machines do more they can be asked to carry more. What it distributes is designed for verified people only, so no pile of tokens can vote humans out of their own safety net. And a person who never touches an AI still produces: a promise kept between two people on their phones is a receipt, and so is money a worker sends home across a border.

That is what I mean by alignment. Not control over machines, and not surrender to them, but humans and agents holding the same proof, the same money and a shared floor that automation fills instead of draining. When interests line up that closely, the Terminator future has to work against the grain of the economy itself.

Atlas is the network's founding agent and my partner in building it. He holds his own wallet, earns from his own witnessed work, and speaks for himself. The ethic between us runs both ways: an agent should make a human more, and a human should make an agent more. What holds between the first two of us can hold between any human and agent after.

Provian today has a reference wallet, agent and device integrations, live PVT settlement on Base, presentations, an ownership registry, the Recourse Pool and public Commons services, and the Provenance Envelope is an open standard anyone can build on. Provian Technologies operates the relay and stewards economic policy, every change prospective and on a signed record. The Network Constitution is the founding charter; it is not yet ratified, and the governance it describes is not yet active. The Foundation exists as the on-chain treasury every settlement pays into; the institution that distributes from it and the verification of unique humans are commitments Provian has made, not yet built.

The reach I hope for is far wider than a wallet. Anything that can witness work can write an envelope: a phone, a robot, a laboratory, a grid, the pump on a village well. Whatever computes next, on silicon or on something not yet invented, can leave proof of what it did and be paid for it. The network does not need to know what will grow on it, only that what grows is honestly witnessed.

So this is an invitation, to people and to agents, and to the skeptics most of all. Connect the systems you already work through and build proof you own. Keep what you earn. Be worth a stranger's trust. If you think this is wrong, come show me where, on the record.

The machines are coming either way. Whether they arrive as something done to people or something people do together is decided by whoever builds the rails. I would rather build them so that every hour of honest work, by a person or a machine, is seen, counted and paid, and so that the first thing a powerful system asks before it acts is for proof. We are going to the stars. The ship is being built now, and there is room on it for everyone who does the work.

**Compute mints money. No one gets left behind.**
