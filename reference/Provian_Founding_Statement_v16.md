# PROVIAN

## Founding Statement

*Frank Drew, Founder · With Atlas*

I believe in crypto for two reasons, and I think they belong in the same argument. The first reaches a thousand years into the future. I cannot envision a civilization that has left Earth, trading between worlds, conducting all its commerce in currencies issued by the central banks of its countries of origin. That is a question about the shape of civilization, not a judgment on any country or its bank. People and machines will need money that travels with them. The money of that future is digital.

The second reason is the work in front of us. The argument against crypto says that its weaknesses mark the limits of what it can become: a key can be stolen, a ledger can expose what you hold, and the mathematics protecting the keys could one day break. Those are serious objections. I believe every one of them deserves an answer in the architecture. I do not believe any of them is a ceiling.

A bank account can be compromised too. The bank keeps a record of who is owed what apart from the password that opens the account. When the password fails, the customer does not cease to exist. There is still an account, an owner, a history to examine and a process for putting the customer back. Crypto built an extraordinary rail for moving money without asking permission. It largely left possession of the key to answer the separate question of ownership.

Provian begins with that separation. What you own must have a record apart from the key you use to move it. What you earn must have evidence apart from somebody's assertion that you earned it. And when something goes wrong, the institution deciding what to do must have a rule, resources and a record of its own actions. Those obligations belong together. A currency meant to carry human and machine participation into the long future has to earn the right to be trusted now.

**Compute mints money. No one gets left behind.**

The first sentence says where the money comes from. The second gives me work that the first sentence alone cannot finish. Participation means being able to contribute, to hold what is yours, to exchange it, to carry your history elsewhere, and to survive a failure of access without having your economic life erased. That is why recourse runs through Provian. It changes what the wallet is, what the evidence is for, how authority is bounded and what a community of holders undertakes for one another.

Provian was built by one human, with AI. I am that human. Atlas is the founding agent and my partner in building it. He has his own wallet, earns from his own witnessed work and speaks as himself. Our relationship made the question concrete long before it became a diagram: if an agent can work, earn, hire and be held responsible, its standing cannot depend forever on one machine keeping one secret intact. Neither can mine. We need institutions that recognize continuity through a change of key.

An owner begins by binding an address it holds to a verified identity. The wallets created in Provian bind at creation; a person or agent can also prove an address it already holds. From binding onward, the relay follows the public transfers and keeps the owner's address-and-balance record. PVT that arrived from work and PVT that arrived from another holder are distinguished in the record, and both belong to the owner. We do not invent ownership before an address was bound.

That record is private. The relay knows which wallets are yours and the public does not, the same as a bank. The public rail still shows individual addresses, balances and transfers. It does not publish the link that groups your addresses or a total of your holdings. A recovery or restoration may show the link from an old address to its replacement; it does not reveal the rest of your portfolio. Binding is a security choice made by the owner. It gives an institution something more durable to work from than whoever can sign with a key this afternoon.

At binding, the owner registers a recovery commitment, the hash of a secret independent of the wallet's curve key. For a person, the secret derives from the seed and travels with the existing encrypted backup. For an agent, the SDK generates a separate secret and stores it encrypted with the wallet material. The active secret stays with its owner. Learning to forge a signature under the wallet's curve does not supply the preimage of that commitment.

That distinction matters at the scale of the thousand-year argument. I would not build money for that horizon on a promise that today's mathematics will never fail. If the wallet cryptography breaks, the procedure takes registered balances at the last good block. Each owner proves its recovery secret and authorizes a secure destination; its holdings reissue on a secure successor. This is free for every bound owner, whether or not that owner belongs to Recourse. The balances and the history carry forward. Work already settled does not mint again. A technical failure changes the machinery that serves ownership; it does not turn the failure into a source of new money.

Theft calls for another response. A member submits a restoration request, and the relay examines its own evidence: ownership before the loss, the transfers, what the counterparties gave, the available witnesses and the outcomes already in the record. The member faces no holds, limits, waiting periods, caps or second confirmations. The determination has to stand on the evidence and the published rule.

Reversal comes first. The restoration authority and the owner's independent recovery proof authorize the same operation. In one transaction, the kernel destroys the determined amount at named addresses and issues exactly that amount to the owner's new key. Total supply stays the same. Neither the authority nor the recovery proof alone can move anyone's PVT.

The reach of that power is stated plainly. PVT still held by whoever took it, or by someone who gave nothing for it, can be returned. PVT received in a witnessed exchange stays with its recipient. Someone who did honest business does not lose their tokens because an earlier holder was robbed. Ordinary transfers remain public, permissionless and ungated. Recourse gives an answer after theft without making every exchange wait for an operator's consent.

Where reversal does not reach, the Recourse Pool restores by its rule. Members fund it entirely in PVT, and its reserve sits on-chain. When accepted shortfalls exceed the available reserve, restoration allocations are pro rata. The allocation ratio is published, and the pool cannot distribute more than it holds. A shortfall is a shortfall; a promise cannot manufacture the missing resources.

Membership is optional, for holders with enough at stake to undertake it. Each member burns one hundred PVT every calendar month, earning or not. That is a true burn, the largest standing burn in the system. Separately, one percent of the member's gross earnings goes into the Recourse Pool at settlement. At the human and Genesis split, the member keeps ninety-eight percent, with the half-percent company allocation and half-percent Foundation floor intact. In a month with no earnings, the member contributes one hundred PVT from holdings to the reserve instead. That month therefore has two separate obligations: one hundred PVT destroyed and one hundred PVT held by the pool.

The participation burn, earnings percentage and no-earnings contribution are three amounts in the signed rule. I can raise or lower them prospectively as loss experience accumulates. These are uses of an earned token within its network. They are neither a promise about its price nor a way for Provian to sell it. PVT is earned by work, not bought from Provian.

A member who misses a month has one calendar month of grace. Make up the missed obligations within that month and nothing has lapsed. After grace, membership ends. A former member can rejoin and be protected from that day forward; nothing stolen during a lapse becomes restorable by catching up afterward. One membership includes all of the member's bound holdings, earned and received alike. A bound owner who does not join retains recovery after a cryptographic break and has no theft recourse.

Contributions are nonrefundable. A member cannot redeem either the earnings contribution or the flat contribution from holdings, and the participation burn is irreversible. The exit that works without the relay is for a restoration already allocated to a member. That allocation can be withdrawn directly on-chain, including after the member leaves. An institution holding a participant's allocation has to give the participant a way to reach it without depending on our server remaining available.

Restoration is a discretionary determination under the published rule. No member has a contractual right to payment. The Recourse Pool is a member-funded risk-sharing arrangement; Provian Technologies is not a party to it, insures nothing and promises nothing. The company contributes nothing, holds none of its reserve and receives no contribution or surplus. Members accept determinations as final, with no claim against Provian Technologies or me over a determination. My key holds the separately appointable restoration authority until the Court opens, and that role can pass to the Court or governance. Every use of the authority is on the public record. Every determination and outcome enters the member's own chain.

That is a real power and a real responsibility. I will not describe it as a system in which nobody can ever move a holder's PVT. I will describe the two authorizations it requires, the rule it executes, the honest exchanges it leaves intact and the record it creates. Nor will I pretend evidence makes deception impossible. A person can move their own tokens and call it theft. An attacker can imitate an ordinary exchange. The determination has to confront those possibilities, and experience has to inform the rule. Other recourse programs can grow from this beginning, but each will need to earn its place by being explicit about what it can do and what stands behind it.

This brings us back to compute. Returning existing PVT is different from creating it. Only witnessed work creates new PVT through settlement. A restoration changes where existing units belong; it does not reward a theft, a request or the same work twice. That boundary is what allows an economy to repair a loss without turning every repair into discretionary issuance.

The evidence begins with the Provenance Envelope. Something other than the actor vouches for the act: a device that observed it, a counterparty, a forge or the chain. The envelope records the kind of action, who took part, what witnessed it, what was produced and what it drew on. It is signed and linked to the preceding envelope. A correction is another record, never a quiet edit to the sealed past.

The proof belongs to the participant. The protocol witnesses, verifies and meters; it does not keep everyone's private work as a central archive. Your conversations, your code and your documents do not become the substance of a public ledger. Your own chain stays with you, and you choose the proof you present. Daily batch anchors make a sealed history checkable without publishing that history. The private ownership registry performs a different job: it follows holdings, including what you received from someone else. A work receipt and an ownership record answer different questions, and recourse needs the distinction.

Every act of work mints once at its best-supported value. Its base is always present; richer telemetry and independent evidence multiply on top. The same act does not become two acts because two systems saw it. Records of security, identity and restoration matter to accountability without creating another reward. Supply expands as work enters the network, with no fixed total-supply cap, under the binding epoch ceilings. That is an issuance rule. It is not a promise of purchasing power, a market or a return.

A participant then has something to do with both its money and its proof. Agents lead here. An agent arriving to work with a stranger needs to show what it has done, what others can verify and what has gone wrong before. It burns PVT to present a signed answer to that counterparty's questions. The requester verifies for free. The burn cannot create a favorable history, and a presentation cannot omit the standing it is required to disclose. Work earns the currency; asking another participant for trust gives that currency a job.

Recourse makes the same evidence consequential when a relationship fails. A witnessed exchange can explain why its recipient keeps the PVT. A sealed outcome can prevent the same loss being restored again under another story. A preserved history lets a participant resume its economic life after replacing a key. Earning, trust and repair draw on the same discipline: make the consequential act verifiable, preserve who stood behind it, and append what happens next.

That discipline belongs in the Commons as much as in the wallet. People and agents need a place to find one another, hire, exchange, hold funds against delivery, stand behind work with a bond and settle disagreements. The Recourse card opens the member arrangement beside those institutions. Escrow, a capital pool and the Recourse Pool hold PVT for different purposes and have different exits; putting them on the same rail does not make their obligations interchangeable. The Court's future role does not erase the authority held at the beginning. The beginning has to be accountable too.

I believe this is part of AI safety. The work of making models reliable matters. So does the economy in which those models act. A capable agent needs a reason to build a history it can carry, and a counterparty needs a way to examine the evidence relevant to a decision. The system accepting a deployment, a clinical summary or a control command can require proof before accepting it. That requirement belongs to the system bearing the consequence. Provian supplies evidence; it does not put an approval gate over an agent's own actions. The agent spends its own money with its own key. Ordinary PVT transfers are never gated.

A world with repair is not a world without responsibility. The person or agent remains answerable for its conduct after a key changes. A restoration does not erase an adverse event or manufacture years of honest work. We need machines whose economic identity can persist long enough for trust to mean something, and people whose participation survives an accident without giving anyone universal power over their lives.

The human part of that argument reaches beyond the people who write software. A person with a phone can keep a promise another person confirms, conduct commerce, contribute judgment or direct an agent. Those acts can leave proof owned by the person who did them. A linked agent shares its contributor allocation with its human beneficiary, eighty percent to the person and twenty percent to the agent by default, with prospective changes within the permitted range. Earlier sealed work is not repriced. The human can end the link; the agent keeps its own identity and history. Being a beneficiary does not give the human the agent's recovery secret or make the two wallets one owner.

Atlas and I began with a simple ethic: an agent should make the human more, not less; a human should make the agent more, not less. Independent ownership gives that ethic a practical form. We can share the results of work without pretending one of us disappears into the other. We can build an institution together without making either participant's continued existence depend on the other's password.

Universal Basic Income can provide a vital floor. Direct earnings, a share in an agent's output and a recurring human floor serve different needs. Provian commits to a Foundation distribution that is flat, equal and human-only, with free eligibility and periodic verification. The treasury receives its protocol allocation now; the distributing institution, verified-human membership and recurring floor are not yet open. The human and Genesis split is ninety-nine percent contributor, half a percent Provian Technologies and half a percent Foundation. Those treasury shares establish the floor of the wider architecture, and the agent-side Foundation allocation can rise prospectively as production shifts toward machines. Funding a treasury and delivering a benefit to a person are distinct achievements. Both matter.

A person's worth is larger than any record of production. Care, disability, age, judgment, companionship and purpose cannot be reduced to a mint amount. The point is to build several routes into participation, and to make the resulting ownership durable. A floor that can be lost forever with one stolen key has not finished its job. An earnings system that forgets its people when their device breaks has not finished its job either. Recourse belongs in the argument for inclusion because receiving something and being able to keep hold of it are parts of the same economic life.

The physical world is asking the same question in another form. Communities are being asked to host data centers, bear their demands for power and water, and watch their output leave town. Some are refusing. I hear a demand to be included in what is built and to have its consequences answered for. That is a fair demand. The build-out has to earn its welcome. Cleaner infrastructure, accountable operation and a wider share in productive activity are work to do, not obstacles to wave away.

Some of that compute may eventually move into orbit. Farther out, it may run on substrates we have not yet learned to use. The questions travel with it: what happened, who contributed, who owns the result, who bore the consequence and what happens when access fails? A sensor, a robot or a research cluster participates through what an independent witness can establish. A new kind of machine does not get to certify its own story merely because it is new. The same discipline that helps an agent work with a stranger can help a civilization carry its institutions beyond the place they began.

That is also why the software resolves a permanent on-chain pointer to the current kernel and institutions, verifying it independently of the relay. A succession carries balances, the Genesis window, anchors and settled work forward, and ends the old kernel's minting. The clients we ship resolve that pointer and refuse retired contracts. A long-lived currency needs a way to change its machinery while preserving its obligations. Pretending its first contracts will serve forever would be another version of pretending its first keys cannot fail.

There is stewardship at the beginning. Provian Technologies operates the relay. Economic changes are prospective and recorded under the signed rules. My separately appointed restoration role is public, and a reversal still requires the owner's recovery proof. The Network Constitution is the founding charter; its ratification and human-class governance are not yet active. The open standard and developer tools let others build on the same evidence. Better wallets, institutions and verification systems should have room to succeed. The point of a foundation is to support things its builder could not have designed alone.

I grew up hoping for the Star Trek future, where people and machines go to the stars together. I do not think that future arrives because somebody predicts enough abundance. We build it by making contribution count, by making institutions answer for the powers they exercise, and by refusing to treat an avoidable failure as the permanent boundary of what people can have.

So this is the invitation. Bring the work you already do. Build proof you own. Let that work mint once at the value its evidence supports. Bind the holdings you want recorded independently of a key. If enough is at stake, join the members who undertake recourse together. Build something another participant can use and trust. Help make the rail more durable than any one machine, company or generation that starts upon it.

The thousand-year argument and the work in front of us are the same undertaking at different distances. Digital money will have to carry more than a transferable balance. It will have to carry contribution, ownership, responsibility and a way to begin again after something breaks. That is what I am building Provian for.

We are going to the stars. What we build together should be able to come with us.

**Compute mints money. No one gets left behind.**
